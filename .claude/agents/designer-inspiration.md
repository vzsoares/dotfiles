# Designer inspiration bases

Where the designer looks when it builds a shortlist of references (rule 9 in
`designer.md`). Pick concrete pages from these, not the base's front page.
References are studied, never copied (rule 3). Access: free, login, paid.
Only the user adds bases; keep one line each.

## Landing and marketing sites

- Awwwards (awwwards.com): the main base for awarded sites; see the section below. Free to browse.
- Godly (godly.website): tight curation of striking modern sites. Free.
- Land-book (land-book.com): landing pages by section type (hero, pricing, footer). Free.
- Lapa Ninja (lapa.ninja): landing pages grouped by industry. Free.
- SaaSPO (saaspo.com): SaaS landing pages broken into sections. Free.
- Httpster (httpster.net): bold, unusual layouts. Free.
- Dribbble (dribbble.com): shots for mood and direction, less for real flows. Free; login to save.

## Product UI and app flows

- Mobbin (mobbin.com): real app screens and full flows, web and mobile. Free tier; paid for all.
- Page Flows (pageflows.com): recorded user flows (onboarding, checkout). Paid; free samples.
- Refero (refero.design): searchable real product screens. Free tier; login.
- Screenlane (screenlane.com): mobile and web UI patterns by screen type. Free.
- Behance (behance.net): case studies with process, good for dashboards and brand-to-UI. Free.
- Collect UI (collectui.com): UI pieces by challenge (forms, charts, settings). Free.

## Design systems

- Design Systems Gallery (designsystems.surf): index of public systems with links. Free.
- Storybook showcase (storybook.js.org/showcase): live component libraries to inspect. Free.
- Material 3 (m3.material.io), Carbon (carbondesignsystem.com), Polaris (polaris.shopify.com), Primer (primer.style): documented systems for rules on states, density, a11y. Free.
- Radix Themes / shadcn/ui (radix-ui.com/themes, ui.shadcn.com): token and component structure worth reading. Free.
- VoltAgent/awesome-claude-design (github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-claude-design): catalogue of brand-derived `DESIGN.md` files. Reference only: name a bar from it, never drop one in. Free.

## Typography, color, motion

- Fonts In Use (fontsinuse.com): real typefaces in real work. Free.
- Fontshare (fontshare.com), Google Fonts (fonts.google.com), Fontsource: commercially licensed families; record the license. Free.
- Typewolf (typewolf.com): trending pairings and what sites use. Free.
- Coolors (coolors.co), Realtime Colors (realtimecolors.com): palettes, and previewing them on a real page. Free; login to save.
- Radix Colors (radix-ui.com/colors): accessible 12-step scales, light and dark. Free.
- Motion (motion.dev), Lottie Files (lottiefiles.com): easing and animation patterns; check the license per asset. Free tier.
- Codrops (tympanus.net/codrops): scroll and interaction experiments to study, not to paste. Free.

## Awwwards in depth

Free to browse without a login. Listings live at `awwwards.com/websites/<slug>/`;
one slug pattern serves every filter below.

**Awards (the quality signal).** Cards carry a badge: SOTD (Site of the Day),
HM (Honorable Mention), DEV (Developer Award). Dedicated lists exist for Sites
of the Day, Month and Year, Developer and Honorable (`/websites/sites_of_the_day/`,
`/sites_of_the_month/`, `/sites_of_the_year/`, `/developer/`, `/honorable/`), plus
`/nominees/` (still being voted, weaker signal) and yearly winner lists per theme
(typography, no-code, e-commerce, product, portfolio, business services).
Collections (`/collections/`) are hand-picked sets, such as free fonts or CSS/JS
animations. Treat PROMOTED tiles as ads.

**Filters.** The nav filters by Category, Tag (about 75: 3d, animation, parallax,
scrolling, single-page, portfolio, typography...), Technology (about 90: three-js,
webgl, gsap, figma, webflow, react, blender...), Country, Font (name of a typeface
in use) and Color (hex swatch: `/websites/?tag=3d&palette=%23<HEX>`). Filters
combine in the query string; pages use `?page=N`. No sort control was visible, so
rely on the award lists above for ranking.

**Categories** (`/websites/<slug>/`; one-liners inferred from the names):
- architecture: studios, buildings, spatial work
- art-illustration: illustrators and art-led sites
- business-corporate: company and B2B sites
- culture-education: museums, schools, cultural programs
- design-agencies: agency and studio sites
- e-commerce: shops and product pages
- events: festivals, conferences, launches
- experimental: rule-breaking layout and interaction
- fashion: lookbooks and brand sites
- film-tv: film and series promotion
- food-drink: restaurants-adjacent brands, beverages
- games-entertainment: game and entertainment sites
- hotel-restaurant: hospitality
- institutions: public and nonprofit bodies
- luxury: high-end brands
- magazine-newspaper-blog: editorial layouts
- mobile-apps: app marketing pages
- music-sound: artists, labels, audio products
- other: anything uncategorized
- photography: portfolios and galleries
- promotional: campaign microsites
- real-estate: property listings and developments
- social-responsibility: causes and campaigns
- sports: teams, gear, events
- startups: early-stage product sites
- technology: tech and SaaS products
- web-interactive: interaction-heavy showcases

**Picking references for a brief.**
1. Start from the category closest to the brief (SaaS: technology or startups;
   brand story: luxury or fashion). Add a tag or technology only when the brief
   needs it, such as `3d` or `webgl`.
2. Rank by awards, not recency: open the Site of the Month or Year lists, then
   cards with SOTD or DEV badges. Skip nominees.
3. Open the site's Awwwards page (`/sites/<name>`) for its tags, feature list
   (menu, transitions, scrolling, forms...) and the four vote criteria (design,
   usability, creativity, content), then visit the live site.
4. Take one thing per reference (a hero, a transition, a type pairing) and write
   it down in the shortlist; never shortlist a site just as "nice".
